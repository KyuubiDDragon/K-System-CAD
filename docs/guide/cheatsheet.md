# Cheatsheet

The Cheatsheet is a comprehensive quick reference guide designed to help users access important codes, contact numbers, and operational procedures at a glance. This tool is particularly useful for emergency services, dispatch operations, and any organization requiring standardized communication protocols.

::: tip Quick Access
Access the Cheatsheet anytime via `/cheatsheet` in the navigation menu. It's available to all authorized users and can be customized for your organization's specific needs.
:::

## Overview

The Cheatsheet provides instant access to critical information that team members need during daily operations:

- **10-Codes** - Standard radio communication codes
- **Emergency Numbers** - Quick contact directory for all services
- **Response Codes** - Priority levels and response protocols
- **Vehicle Types** - Equipment capabilities and purposes
- **Work Groups/Ranks** - Organizational hierarchy
- **NATO Alphabet** - Phonetic alphabet for clear communication
- **Building Schema** - Location reference system

The interface displays all information in organized, easy-to-read cards that can be quickly scanned during active operations.

## Response Codes

Response codes indicate the priority and urgency of a call:

| Code | Description | When to Use |
|------|-------------|-------------|
| **Code 1** | No acute emergency | Routine calls, non-urgent matters |
| **Code 2** | Silent and faster approach | Time-sensitive but not critical |
| **Code 3** | Critical / Unknown | Emergency situations, lights and sirens |
| **Code 4** | Operation completed | Scene secured, incident resolved |
| **Code 6** | Arrived at scene | Confirming arrival at incident location |

### Guidelines

- **Code 1**: Standard response without emergency equipment activated
- **Code 2**: Expedited response but maintaining public safety (no sirens)
- **Code 3**: Full emergency response with lights and sirens
- **Code 4**: Used to inform dispatch that scene is under control
- **Code 6**: Confirmation of arrival for dispatch tracking

## Vehicle Types

Understanding vehicle capabilities and primary purposes:

### Engine
**Purpose:** Fire suppression, traffic accidents, interior attack

Engines are the primary firefighting vehicles equipped with water pumps, hoses, and basic rescue equipment. They respond to most fire calls and are first-line units for structure fires.

### Tower
**Purpose:** Fire suppression, interior attack, high-angle rescue support

Tower ladders provide elevated water streams and access to upper floors. They support interior operations and can assist with high-angle rescue scenarios.

### Rescue
**Purpose:** Diving operations, high/low angle rescue, technical assistance, Hazmat operations

Specialized rescue vehicles carry technical equipment for complex rescue operations including:
- Water rescue and diving
- High-angle and confined space rescue
- Technical extrication
- Hazardous materials response

### Fleet
**Vehicle Types:**
- **Fleet 3**: Flatbed truck (equipment transport)
- **Fleet 1 & 2**: Tow trucks (vehicle recovery)

Support vehicles for equipment transport and vehicle towing operations.

### Firehawk
**Aircraft Types:**
- **Firehawk 3**: Fire suppression helicopter
- **Firehawk 1 & 2**: Rescue helicopters

Aerial units provide:
- Water/retardant drops for wildland fires
- Aerial reconnaissance
- Remote area rescue
- Emergency medical transport

## Building Schema

The building schema provides a standardized system for describing locations within structures:

### Side Designation

Buildings are divided into four sides using the NATO alphabet:

- **Alpha** - Street side (typically main entrance, often easiest access)
- **Bravo** - Right side when facing Alpha (clockwise from Alpha)
- **Charlie** - Rear side (opposite of Alpha)
- **Delta** - Left side when facing Alpha (completing the clockwise circuit)

### Floor Designation

Floors are numbered as divisions:

- **Division Roof** - Roof level
- **Division 4** - 4th floor
- **Division 3** - 3rd floor
- **Division 2** - 2nd floor
- **Division 1** - Ground floor/First floor
- **Division 0** - Basement

### Communication Examples

**"Division 2, Alpha side"** = Second floor, street side
**"Division Roof, Charlie"** = Roof level, rear of building
**"Division 0, Delta side"** = Basement, left side

::: tip Best Practice
Always use the building schema when communicating locations during structure fires or complex incidents. This ensures all personnel know exact locations without confusion.
:::

## Tips and Best Practices

### Memorization Techniques

1. **Daily Review**: Take 5 minutes each shift to review codes
2. **Practical Use**: Use codes in non-emergency communications to build familiarity
3. **Study Groups**: Quiz each other on codes and procedures
4. **Flashcards**: Create physical or digital flashcards for quick study

### Communication Best Practices

**Be Clear and Concise**
- Use codes appropriately but don't overuse them
- Speak slowly and clearly over radio
- Repeat critical information (addresses, unit numbers)

**Confirm Receipt**
- Always acknowledge important messages with "10-4" or verbal confirmation
- Repeat back addresses and critical details

**Use NATO Alphabet**
- Spell out license plates, names, and addresses using phonetic alphabet
- Especially important in noisy environments or poor radio conditions

**Know Your Numbers**
- Memorize the top 5 emergency services numbers
- Keep this cheatsheet accessible during operations
- Program frequently used numbers in your devices

### When to Use Which Codes

**10-Codes**: For routine radio communications and status updates
**Response Codes**: When describing call priority or scene status
**NATO Alphabet**: When spelling anything over radio or phone
**Building Schema**: During structure fires and multi-story incidents

### Training Integration

- Include cheatsheet review in new member orientation
- Test code knowledge during quarterly evaluations
- Update organizational codes as procedures change
- Share real-world scenarios where codes prevented confusion

## Admin Management

::: tip Fully Editable
The Cheatsheet is now fully editable through the admin interface. Authorized administrators can add, edit, and remove categories and items without touching any code.
:::

### Access Admin Interface

**Route:** `/admin/cheatsheet`
**Permission Required:** `ADMIN_CHEATSHEET`

Administrators with the appropriate permissions can access the Cheatsheet management interface to:

### Managing Categories

**Create New Categories:**
1. Click "Add Category" in the admin interface
2. Configure category settings:
   - **Name**: Display name for the category
   - **Description**: Optional description
   - **Icon**: Material Design Icon (e.g., `mdi-police-badge`)
   - **Color**: Theme color (`primary`, `error`, `success`, etc.)
   - **Content Type**: Choose from:
     - `table` - Two-column table layout (title/description)
     - `grid` - Multi-column grid (like NATO alphabet)
     - `text` - Text-based content with paragraphs
     - `image` - Image with caption and description
     - `mixed` - Combination of multiple content types
   - **Width**: Display width (`full`, `half`, `third`, `quarter`)
   - **Height**: Maximum height (`auto`, `small`, `medium`, `large`)
   - **Enabled**: Toggle visibility to users

**Edit Categories:**
- Click the pencil icon next to any category
- Update any settings
- Changes apply immediately

**Delete Categories:**
- Click the delete icon (warning: this removes all items in the category)

### Managing Items

**Add Items to a Category:**
1. Click "Manage Items" (list icon) for the category
2. Click "Add Item"
3. Fill in the item details:
   - **Title**: Primary text (e.g., "10-4")
   - **Description**: Secondary text (e.g., "Acknowledged")
   - **Content Text**: Optional longer text content
   - **Image URL**: Optional image path
   - **Enabled**: Toggle visibility

**Edit Items:**
- Click the pencil icon next to any item
- Update details as needed

**Delete Items:**
- Click the delete icon to remove an item

### Import/Export

**Export a Category:**
1. Click the download icon next to a category
2. JSON file downloads with category and all items
3. Use for backups or sharing configurations

**Import a Category:**
1. Click "Import" button
2. Paste JSON data from exported file
3. Click "Import" to add the category
4. System automatically creates category and all items

### Content Types Explained

**Table Type:**
Best for lists with title/description pairs:
- 10-Codes
- Emergency Numbers
- Response Codes
- Vehicle Types

**Grid Type:**
Multi-column layout for compact data:
- NATO Alphabet (2 columns showing A-M, N-Z)

**Image Type:**
Visual reference with text:
- Building Schema diagrams
- Maps and charts
- Equipment diagrams

**Text Type:**
Paragraph-based content:
- Procedures
- Guidelines
- Policy summaries

**Mixed Type:**
Combination of images, text, and structured data for complex content

### Authority-Specific Customization

Each authority (organization) has its own independent cheatsheet data:
- Changes only affect your organization
- Different authorities can have completely different categories
- Seed data is automatically created for new authorities
